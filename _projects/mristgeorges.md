The project aims to develop an AI system to assist in diagnosing primary lymphoedema, a condition causing chronic swelling due to lymphatic system defects. Diagnosis currently uses lymphoscintigraphy, an imaging technique where a radioactive tracer shows how lymph fluid moves. While scans reveal distinct patterns in different patient groups, interpreting them accurately requires expert review.

In the first phase, researchers will identify key image features to train a computer model to classify scans. Later, they plan to develop a **neurosymbolic AI** approach that combines pattern recognition from neural networks with symbolic reasoning about lymphatic anatomy. This hybrid method could make the system both more accurate and interpretable, reasoning about structures in ways similar to human experts.

The work, funded by the Medical Research Council (MRC) and British Heart Foundation (BHF), will form the basis for future collaborations to develop this advanced diagnostic tool.
